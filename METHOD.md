# Bound Referrals

*The mechanism. Named and dated so it can be cited: Bound Referral, Jodi
Paige-Lee, September 2026.*

Most of what matters under this law cannot be settled from public sources. The
threshold question — whether a tool is even an AEDT — turns on the three prongs
at §5-300: whether the employer relies solely on the tool's output, weights it
above every other criterion, or uses it to overrule conclusions reached by other
means. Those are facts about how an employer runs its process. No amount of
looking at a website settles them.

Absence has the same problem in reverse. You cannot prove a summary is nowhere on
a large site. You can only report where you looked.

The usual move is to write "requires manual review" and move on. That hands a
human a shrug. A **Bound Referral** hands them a question with its consequences
already decided:

```
REF-01  scope determination
provision: AEDT-DEF-SUBST  (rcny-5-300-definitions.md:L23)
question:  Does the employer rely solely on this tool's output, weight it above
           every other criterion, or use it to overrule conclusions reached by
           other means?
evidence:  The employer's own description of how the output enters the hiring
           decision — screening policy, recruiter instructions, vendor contract.
once answered, if YES →  The tool is an AEDT. All four obligations become live
           and are adjudicated on the findings already gathered.
once answered, if NO  →  The law does not apply. Every obligation resolves
           NOT APPLICABLE.
status:    OPEN
```

Three properties make it a mechanism rather than a caveat:

- The outcomes are **committed before the answer arrives**, so a human supplies
  one input rather than re-opening the judgement. Writing the referral decides
  nothing: the branches say what an answer *would* mean, and neither fires until
  a person answers. That is why each one reads `once answered, if…`.
- An open referral **cannot resolve by silence or by time**. It never ages into a
  pass.
- While any referral is open, the report's overall state is
  **AWAITING HUMAN DETERMINATION**. That is not a pass, and the report says so in
  those words. There is no hedged verdict on offer, and the auditor cannot be
  argued into one.

The scope referral blocks everything beneath it. Until it is answered, no
obligation has a verdict at all.

Some referrals are **conditional** — they carry a firing condition and stay
dormant unless a verdict already reached in the report meets it. `REF-03` is the
example: it fires only when the bias-audit summary is missing, and it asks the
one question that decides whether that absence is a breach or a duty that
lawfully expired. Its second branch names, in advance, the fact that would clear
the employer.

That is what makes this an audit rather than a complaint. Most compliance tools
can only find problems. This one commits in advance to the evidence that would
exonerate.
